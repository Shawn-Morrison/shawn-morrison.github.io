# Project Retrospective  
## Project 03 – Trying New Restaurants  
**Shawn Morrison | CPMA-3301**

---

## Introduction

This retrospective reflects on the planning, execution, and delivery of the “Trying New Restaurants” website project. The purpose of this document is to evaluate the process and identify improvements for future projects.

---

## 1. What Went Well?

### Clear Scope Definition

Defining the project scope early was one of the most important successes. By limiting the project to a two-page static website with no JavaScript or advanced features, the work remained manageable within a short timeline. The exclusions section of the scope statement helped prevent unnecessary additions.

This reinforced the importance of clearly defined boundaries before beginning execution.

---

### Structured Planning with TAME

Using the TAME framework to identify risks brought intentionality to the planning process. Even for a small project, identifying time compression, scope creep, and documentation misalignment created awareness and improved decision-making.

The process demonstrated that risk management is valuable even on small-scale projects.

---

### Organized Repository Structure

Keeping documentation inside a `/docs` folder and separating technical files in the root directory created a professional, clean repository. This organization made it easier to verify requirements and ensured nothing was overlooked before submission.

---

### Sequential Work Phases

Completing documentation first and then building the website reduced confusion and rework. Having a defined Work Breakdown Structure helped maintain focus and momentum.

---

## 2. What Went Wrong?

### Time Pressure and Compression

Although the scope was limited, completing the project within a few days created mild time pressure. This reduced the time available for aesthetic refinement and deeper content polishing.

The issue was not caused by technical difficulty but by my own compressed scheduling.

---

### Minor Rework During Development

Some content decisions changed while building the website (for example, adjusting layout structure and visual styling). This resulted in small revisions that could have been avoided with slightly more upfront design planning.

This was not a major issue, but it highlighted the value of drafting structure before implementation.

---

## 3. What Will I Do Differently Next Time?


### Schedule Buffer Time

Even for small projects, I will intentionally schedule buffer time for refinement and quality review. This will reduce time compression and allow more thoughtful polishing.

---

## Conclusion

This project demonstrated that even a small website benefits from structured planning, risk analysis, and formal closure. The most valuable lesson was that disciplined process management improves clarity, efficiency, and final quality—even under short deadlines.

By formally reflecting on what worked and what did not, this project becomes more than a completed assignment. It becomes a repeatable framework for approaching future digital and professional initiatives.
